Project use

From participation to spatial evidence

The poll can support a broader study of campus space use by comparing stated preferences with observations, occupancy counts, and spatial characteristics. It identifies where follow-up interviews or site analysis may be most useful, rather than explaining preference by itself.

Data collected

Minimal, aggregated categories

Firebase stores only a running count for each of six study-space categories. The page does not ask for names, email addresses, demographics, location traces, or written responses. A browser-local marker remembers the selected category so a participant can change rather than stack votes.

Ethics and limits

Anonymous does not mean representative

Participation is voluntary and self-selected, so the results cannot represent all Columbia students. Browser-based duplicate control is only a lightweight safeguard and can be bypassed. A longer-term deployment should use clear consent language, secure rules, limited retention, accessibility review, and a documented plan for who may use the results.
